# 30-day Dimaso distribution playbook

## Primary routes

- Outbound: white-label WordPress and web delivery for agencies.
- Organic/search: nonprofit maintenance and redesign guidance.
- Specialist route: WooCommerce maintenance for revenue-sensitive stores.

## Weekly operating target

- 25 qualified prospects researched.
- 25 personalized first contacts, five per workday.
- Five agency-partner conversations started.
- Two proof-led LinkedIn posts from the founder profile and, when appropriate, the company page.
- One existing client asked for an approved quote, metric, or review.
- Every link tagged with the matching row from `utm-tracking.csv`.

## Qualification rules

Contact an organization only when at least one observable condition exists:

- Agency publicly offers WordPress/web work but appears to need overflow or maintenance capacity.
- Nonprofit has an active donation, event, volunteer, or campaign website with a visible maintenance risk.
- WooCommerce store has a visible checkout, product, performance, integration, or release-complexity signal.

Record the exact page and observation. Do not claim a security, accessibility, SEO, or checkout defect unless it was actually verified.

## Follow-up sequence

- Day 0: personalized observation and one low-friction question.
- Day 4: send one relevant checklist or case study; no generic “checking in.”
- Day 10: close the loop and invite them to reconnect when the workload becomes active.
- Stop after the second follow-up unless the prospect engages.

## Eight LinkedIn drafts

1. **A WordPress update is not complete when the dashboard says success.** Explain priority-page, form, checkout, email, tracking, and rollback checks. Link to WordPress Support.
2. **What agencies should define before using a white-label partner.** Cover client visibility, NDA, access, approvals, QA, handoff, and escalation. Link to Agencies.
3. **The nonprofit maintenance check most teams miss: the complete donation path.** Cover confirmation, email, analytics, mobile, and failure states. Link to the nonprofit checklist.
4. **A WooCommerce store can be online while orders are failing operationally.** Cover pricing, email, inventory, payment, analytics, and fulfillment. Link to WooCommerce Maintenance.
5. **What a useful monthly maintenance report should show.** Completed work, tested journeys, open risk, next priorities, and decisions needed. Link to the reporting article.
6. **A case-study post based on one verified fact.** Use only a fact completed in `client-proof-verification.md`; show context → work → evidence → result.
7. **Why we stopped treating llms.txt as an AI visibility strategy.** Explain that useful evidence, crawlability, structured data, internal links, and measurement come first. Link to Technical SEO or AI Workflow Support.
8. **Send one website problem, not a perfect RFP.** Explain the senior-review intake: website, work email, and one priority. Link to Contact.

Each post should include one first-hand example or screenshot, one practical takeaway, and one tagged link. Do not publish all eight in the same week.

## Success metrics

Track separately:

- Qualified contacts sent.
- Positive replies.
- Workload or website reviews requested.
- Partner calls.
- Proposals.
- UTM-tagged qualified sessions.
- Form submit attempts, errors, successful GA events, and mailbox-confirmed leads.

Do not use impressions, Direct GA4 sessions, or unverified key events as lead evidence.
